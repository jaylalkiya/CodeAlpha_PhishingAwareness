Module 1

What is phishing?

Phishing is when an attacker pretends to be someone you trust (your bank, your boss, a delivery company, IT support) to trick you into clicking a link, opening an attachment, sharing a password or OTP, or sending money.

Why it works: it attacks people, not computers. Firewalls and antivirus can't stop you from typing your password into a convincing fake page.

The attacker's goal is usually one of these:

🔑Steal passwords & OTPs
💸Get you to send money
🦠Install malware or ransomware
🗂Steal personal or company data

Module 3

Fake websites & URL tricks

A fake login page can look pixel-perfect. The one thing an attacker can't fake is the domain name, so learn to read it.

Anatomy of a URL

https://login.northbank.com/account/settings
protocol subdomain (anything the owner wants) registered domain: who you're really talking to path
Rule of thumb: find the first single / after https://. The two words just before it (e.g. northbank.com, or three for .co.in/.co.uk) are the real domain. Everything further left is just decoration.

Common tricks

Typosquatting

n0rthbank.com, northbnak.com, rn instead of m. One wrong character.

Brand in the subdomain

northbank.com.secure-login.net. The real domain is secure-login.net.

Extra words

northbank-support.help, northbank-kyc.in. A different domain, not the bank.

The @ trick

northbank.com@evil.ru. Browsers ignore everything before the @.

Look-alike letters

Cyrillic а vs Latin a. Shown in the address bar as xn-- punycode.

HTTPS ≠ safe

The padlock means the connection is encrypted, not that the site is genuine. Most phishing sites use HTTPS.

Fake login page checklist

  • Did you arrive by clicking a link in a message? Close it and type the address yourself.
  • Does your password manager refuse to autofill? It doesn't recognise the domain. Big warning.
  • Is it asking for more than usual: OTP, CVV, ATM PIN, Aadhaar all on one page?
  • Pop-ups, countdown timers, poor logos or broken links elsewhere on the page.

Module 4

Social engineering tactics

Social engineering is hacking the human. Instead of breaking in, attackers persuade you to open the door. Almost every phishing message pulls on one of these psychological levers. Click a card to see an example.

Spot the feeling: if a message makes you feel rushed, scared, excited or flattered, that's your cue to slow down. Attackers want an emotional reaction, not a considered one.

Beyond email

📞 Vishing calls

"Bank fraud team", "Customs officer", "IT help desk". Hang up and call back on the official number.

💬 Smishing & WhatsApp

Parcel fees, electricity disconnection, KYC updates, "Hi mum, new number". Never pay or click from an SMS.

🚪 Tailgating

Someone with full hands asks you to hold the secure door. Politely ask them to badge in.

💾 Baiting

A USB drive labelled "Salaries 2026" left in the car park. Never plug in unknown devices.
